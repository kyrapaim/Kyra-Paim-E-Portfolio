The Journey

Trip Description and Overview

Where I went, what I worked on, and why it mattered.

Hello, I'm Kyra Paim! I'm pursuing a Computer Science degree with a minor in Brazilian Portuguese at the University of Florida. Over the span of six weeks, I studied abroad in Cape Town, South Africa through the UF International Center's (UFIC) partnership with EDU Africa. This ePortfolio documents the community I worked alongside, the application my team developed, and the personal and professional lessons I carried home.

Over the course of this internship, I worked as a team lead in a collaboration with a local nonprofit organization, the Princess Vlei Forum, to integrate an AI chatbot into their website. As a nonprofit organization, they rely heavily on the local community and volunteers to keep the space vitalized. Building a chatbot for their website allowed for users to easily find out how they can get involved, or facts about the rich biodiversity at the Vlei. We had the privledge of stretching this goal further, and redeveloped their website to save costs and enagage users further. Beyond the technical work, the trip immersed me in South African culture, history, and everyday life. From the stunning view of Table Mountain every morning to the community and friendships I built overseas, this study abroad was one of the most enriching experiences of my life.

This portfolio is made with the intention to reflect on my international engagement and connect it to my academic and professional goals. It serves not only to capture my team's work, but how it changed the way I think about technology, collaboration, and the world.

What We Built

Chatbot and Refactored Website Walkthrough

A visual showcase of the chatbot my team designed and developed during the program.

The Princess Vlei Assistant

The Princess Vlei Assistant is a friendly River Otter, an animal native to the Vlei. The Assistant assists users navigating the website's contents. Along the bottom right corner of the website, the users have the option to speak with the chatbot. Opening the chatbot gives the user the ability to ask preset questions, or personally ask anything in relation to the Vlei.

  1. The Problem

    The old Princess Vlei Forum website was messy and confusing. Users would have a hard time navigating the site to find relevant information quickly. Princess Vlei needed a feature that would boost user engagement and help site visitors find information fast.

  2. Our Approach

    We needed to integrate an AI chatbot into a dated website that depended on a buggy website builder. Working for a nonprofit, we had to make the chatbot free, reliable, and easy to manage for our non-technical clients. Our team applied for a Microsoft Azure Grant for Nonprofits, and succeeded in recieving $2000 of free credits every year for the Princess Vlei Forum. This chatbot was integrated into the original website, and our team rebuilt and transferred the content to a new website. The goal of the refactorting was to take away the website builder dependancy, reduce costs for the organization, and build a cleaner, user-friendly platform to support community engagement.

  3. Key Features

    Our chatbot and new website is cost free! The system prompt limits the DeepSeek model to a certain number of credits a day, and once that credit is reached the Princess Vlei Assistant falls back on the free CloudFlare AI model. This ensures the credit for $2000 worth of tokens isn't exceeded, allowing for the chatbot to continue being cost free for the client but still avaliable at all times. Users can speak with the chatbot to find out more about the Vlei and how they can get involved. People who are curious about wildlife can be linked directly to INaturalist findings of animals at the Vlei and when these sighting were recorded. Refactoring the website and hosting on CloudFlare Pages took away extra subscription costs the client was paying towards editing and hosting their site. To accomodate their nontechnical admin, we built a custon admin page for the Forum's website author to easily upload new articles and information for the chatbot.

  4. Impact

    After 6 weeks, my team built a reliable chatbot assistant focused on answering questions soley about the Vlei. Refactorting the site off of a website builder made Princess Vlei's website engaging, organized, and modern. We built around nontechnical admin, and established a way for the client to have a comfortable transition to a new platform. Overall, the chatbot and new website serves to attract potential visitors and volunteers through an easier user experience.

Week by Week

Lessons Gained Along the Way

Weekly reflections from my internship

Week 1 · Arrival

Adapting to a New Environment

As I am used to traveling, I'm comfortable being in places I'm unfamiliar with. This first week was for exploring and understanding. As a class, we were able to detach from our homes and experience South Africa as global citizens of the world. Guest speaker Aika Swai presented a capturing talk to help us understand the cultural and historical context of the South Africa we were stepping into. It was a pivotal opening for my trip that made me understand to let go of idea of "helping" and take on a role that focuses on listening. Our first client meeting consisted of Princess Vlei staff taking my team for a walking tour through the conservation area. They showed us local and endangered plants and animals and explained the cultural significance of the area. Through this experience, my teammates and I were able to connect with the rich history of the Vlei and understand the purpose of retaining and expanding the community that the Vlei thrives on. Overall, this week served as an introduction into what we were going to build, who was this product meant for, and how we were going to establish ourselves as a team. By the end of the week, we presented our Project Pitch to Dr. Thomas, EDU Africa, and our classmates.

Week 2 · Product Conceptilization

Brain Storming

Conceptilization began with me building wireframes to visualize what we wanted our product to look like. After visiting the Vlei, we noted our client saying that River Otters are native to the area. To make our chatbot more personalized and special to the Vlei, we decided to adapt the River Otter as a persona/icon of the Vlei Assistant. The client was pleased by the idea, and it allowed me to express creativity through my technical work. My team started out with a free CloudFlare AI model to power our chatbot. It was slow and unreliable, but it showed us what we needed to focus on in terms of safety and response content. As a program, we began to explore the city more. I experienced new foods, landscapes, and activities! I began building bonds with my classmates and spending my free time with them. Together, we experienced new things everyday which I believe drew us even closer.

Week 3 · Building

From Idea to Prototype

After recieving the Microsoft Azure for Nonprofits grant, we switched our prototype chatbot model to a efficent, reliable, and cost-effective DeepSeek model. I began constructing a new website and experimenting with how to restructure the visual interface while protecting each page's contents. Our program had the honor of touring Stellenbosch University and sharing a braai with their students. It was such a welcoming and warming experience to get to talk and relate to university students halfway across the world from you. As a team lead, I had the privledge of speaking at this braai to introduce myself, my team, and the project we were building. Our program also visited a local highschool, Curro Delft, and participated with students to compete in a brainstorming contest. We were tasked to collaborate amongst each other to conceptulize and pitch an idea for an application that would help local South African Communities. My group consisted of 2 UF and 3 Curro Delft students, and together our idea won first place. My classmate and I took the time to listen to the students and understand relevant struggles they experience themselves. It taught us as tourists in a foreign country to not create what we think people need, but to attend to people's needs when creating.

Week 4 · Testing

Overcoming Technical Challenges

After integrating the chatbot into the old website, our team tested the chatbots for safety, accuracy, and speed. The clients wanted us to incorporate the ability for the chatbot to give users direct links to INaturalist pages when asking about certain species at the Vlei. INaturalist is a crowdsource platform for capturing photos and locations of wildlife observations. In turn, this source provided relevant information for responses while allowing the chatbot to entice greater user involvement with the local community of the Vlei. As a team, we wanted our chatbot to have consistent responses in style, tone, and relevance. To ensure this, we tested a plethora of questions and conversations to purposefully find irregularties in our product. In addition, this week also focused on the transfer of website content including photos and links. I began working on User Interface (UI) elements and asking the client their opinions on colors, fonts, and layouts. This week, our group visted the Amazon Web Services (AWS) Skills Center in Cape Town. Together, we participated in workshops and a prototyping contest utilizing their Kiro AI IDE tool. I collaborated with a team of peers to brainstorm and leverage AI to bring an idea into fruition. Finalizing our protoype, we presented a product showcase to peers and AWS instructors.

Week 5 · Refining

Feedback & Fixes

Through conducting usability testing with our clients and peers, we understood what it really meant to make user-centered products. Being so used to seeing and using our chatbot everyday, we missed key details our accustomed eyes grew used to. Having a fresh set of eyes gave our team helpful feedback into the visual and technical fixes our product needed. We refined the visuals to advertise and provide a clearer role of the chatbot on the website. Making these changes made users notice the chatbot quicker, and realize its purpose upon first glance. Conducting usability tests for my peers allowed me to take inspiration from their clean and useful design elements. I was able to put myself in the role of a user and navigate their sites with an open mind and no context. Conducting these tests allowed me to catch glitches and irregularties for my peers, which allowed them to fix issues previously unknown to them.

Week 6 · Finalizing

Delivering & Saying Goodbye

As a team, we completed our final presentation showcasing our Princess Vlei Chatbot Assistant, and our prototyped refactored website. Although our program was ending, progress was still left to be made with the website refactor. The Princess Vlei Assistant was finished and avaliable on the old website for the time being. Our clients were pleased with our completed chatbot and the progress of our new website. They felt prioritized when we demonstrated an admin page specifically designed for the nontechnical author of the website. The admin page makes an easier transition to a new website so the client can upload and edit content in a form they are accustomed to. Being so used to using drag and drop website builders, we did not want to overwhelm the client with new technologies. Therefore, we took inspiration from these layouts to build something similar but specific for the new Princess Vlei website and their staff. Overall, saying goodbye to people I was seeing everyday for six weeks was the hardest part. Over time, I grew strong bonds with my peers. Together, we collaborated, explored, and joked. Interning abroad allowed the perfect mix of responsibility, impact, and fun. I was able to collaborate and create a project with a purpose. I was able to show up as both a peer and a friend. Watching everyone else on the program build well-thoughout projects made me proud of the work we completed as a group.

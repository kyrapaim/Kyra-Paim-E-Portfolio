The Journey

Trip Description and Overview

Hello, I'm Kyra Paim! I'm pursuing a Computer Science degree with a minor in Brazilian Portuguese at the University of Florida. Over the span of six weeks, I studied abroad in Cape Town, South Africa through the UF International Center's (UFIC) partnership with EDU Africa.

Over the course of this internship, I worked as a team lead in a collaboration with a local nonprofit organization, the Princess Vlei Forum. My group integrated an AI chatbot into their website to boost engagement with the local community and volunteers that keep the space vitalized. Building a chatbot allowed for users to easily find out how they can get involved, or facts about the rich biodiversity at the Vlei. We had the priviledge of stretching this goal further, and redeveloped their website to save costs and engage users further. Beyond the technical work, the trip immersed me in South African culture, history, and everyday life. From the stunning view of Table Mountain every morning to the community and friendships I built overseas, this study abroad was one of the most enriching experiences of my life.

This portfolio is made with the intention to reflect on my international engagement and connect it to my academic and professional goals. It serves not only to capture my team's work, but how it changed the way I think about technology, collaboration, and the world.

What We Built

Chatbot and Refactored Website Walkthrough

A visual showcase of the chatbot my team designed and developed during the program.

The Princess Vlei Assistant

The Princess Vlei Assistant is a friendly River Otter, an animal native to the Vlei. The Assistant assists users navigating the website's contents. Along the bottom right corner of the website, the users have the option to speak with the chatbot. Opening the chatbot gives the user the ability to ask preset questions, or personally ask anything in relation to the Vlei.

  1. The Problem

    The old Princess Vlei Forum website was messy and confusing. Users would have a hard time navigating the site to find relevant information quickly. Princess Vlei needed a feature that would boost user engagement and help site visitors find information fast.

  2. Our Approach

    We needed to integrate an AI chatbot into a dated website that depended on a buggy website builder. Working for a nonprofit, we had to make the chatbot free, reliable, and easy to manage for our non-technical clients. Our team applied for a Microsoft Azure Grant for Nonprofits, and succeeded in receiving $2000 of free credits every year for the Princess Vlei Forum. This chatbot was integrated into the original website, and our team rebuilt and transferred the content to a new website. The goal of the refactoring was to take away the website builder dependancy, reduce costs for the organization, and build a cleaner, user-friendly platform to support community engagement.

  3. Key Features

    Our chatbot and new website are cost free! The system prompt limits the DeepSeek model to a certain number of credits a day, and once that credit is reached the Princess Vlei Assistant falls back on the free CloudFlare AI model. This ensures the credit for $2000 worth of tokens isn't exceeded, allowing for the chatbot to continue being cost free for the client but still available at all times. Users can speak with the chatbot to find out more about the Vlei and how they can get involved. People who are curious about wildlife can be linked directly to INaturalist findings of animals at the Vlei and when these sightings were recorded. Refactoring the website and hosting on CloudFlare Pages took away extra subscription costs the client was paying towards editing and hosting their site. To accommodate our nontechnical client, we built a custom admin page for the Forum's website author to easily upload new articles and information for the chatbot.

  4. Impact

    After 6 weeks, my team built a reliable chatbot assistant focused on answering questions solely about the Vlei. Refactoring the site off of a website builder made Princess Vlei's website engaging, organized, and modern. We built around nontechnical admin, and established a way for the client to have a comfortable transition to a new platform. Overall, the chatbot and new website serves to attract potential visitors and volunteers through an easier user experience.

Week by Week

Lessons Gained Along the Way

Weekly reflections from my internship

Week 1 · Arrival

Adapting to a New Environment

As I am used to traveling, I'm comfortable being in places I'm unfamiliar with. This first week was for exploring and understanding. As a class, we were able to detach from our homes and experience South Africa as global citizens of the world. Guest speaker Aika Swai presented a capturing talk to help us understand the cultural and historical context of the South Africa we were stepping into. It was a pivotal opening for my trip that made me understand how to let go of the idea of "helping" and take on a role that focuses on listening. Our first client meeting consisted of Princess Vlei staff taking my team for a walking tour through the conservation area. They showed us local and endangered plants and animals and explained the cultural significance of the area. Through this experience, my teammates and I were able to connect with the rich history of the Vlei and understand the purpose of retaining and expanding the community that the Vlei thrives on. Overall, this week served as an introduction into what we were going to build, who this product meant for, and how we were going to establish ourselves as a team. By the end of the week, we presented our Project Pitch to Dr. Thomas, EDU Africa, and our classmates.

Week 2 · Product Conceptilization

Brain Storming

Conceptilization began with me building wireframes to visualize what we wanted our product to look like. After visiting the Vlei, we noted our client saying that River Otters are native to the area. To make our chatbot more personalized and special to the Vlei, we decided to adapt the River Otter as a persona/icon of the Vlei Assistant. The client was pleased by the idea, and it allowed me to express creativity through my technical work. My team started out with a free CloudFlare AI model to power our chatbot. It was slow and unreliable, but it showed us what we needed to focus on in terms of safety and response content. As a program, we began to explore the city more. I experienced new foods, landscapes, and activities! I began building bonds with my classmates and spending my free time with them. Together, we experienced new things everyday which I believe drew us even closer.

Week 3 · Building

From Idea to Prototype

After receiving the Microsoft Azure for Nonprofits grant, we switched our prototype chatbot model to an efficent, reliable, and cost-effective DeepSeek model. I began constructing a new website and experimenting with how to restructure the visual interface while protecting each page's contents. Our program had the honor of touring Stellenbosch University and sharing a braai with their students. It was such a welcoming and warming experience to get to talk and relate to university students halfway across the world from you. As a team lead, I had the privilege of speaking at this braai to introduce myself, my team, and the project we were building. Our program also visited a local highschool, Curro Delft, and participated with students to compete in a brainstorming contest. We were tasked to collaborate amongst each other to conceptualize and pitch an idea for an application that would help local South African Communities. My group consisted of 2 UF and 3 Curro Delft students, and together our idea won first place. My classmate and I took the time to listen to the students and understand relevant struggles they experience themselves. It taught us as tourists in a foreign country to not create what we think people need, but to attend to people's needs when creating.

Week 4 · Testing

Testing Early and Often

After integrating the chatbot into the old website, our team tested the chatbots for safety, accuracy, and speed. The clients wanted us to incorporate the ability for the chatbot to give users direct links to INaturalist pages when asking about certain species at the Vlei. INaturalist is a crowdsource platform for capturing photos and locations of wildlife observations. In turn, this source provided relevant information for responses while allowing the chatbot to entice greater user involvement with the local community of the Vlei. As a team, we wanted our chatbot to have consistent responses in style, tone, and relevance. To ensure this, we tested a plethora of questions and conversations to purposefully find irregularities in our product. In addition, this week also focused on the transfer of website content including photos and links. I began working on User Interface (UI) elements and asking the client their opinions on colors, fonts, and layouts. This week, our group visited the Amazon Web Services (AWS) Skills Center in Cape Town. Together, we participated in workshops and a prototyping contest utilizing their Kiro AI IDE tool. I collaborated with a team of peers to brainstorm and leverage AI to bring an idea into fruition. After finalizing our prototype, we presented a product showcase to peers and AWS instructors.

Week 5 · Refining

Feedback & Fixes

Through conducting usability testing with our clients and peers, we understood what it really meant to make user-centered products. Being so used to seeing and using our chatbot everyday, we missed key details our accustomed eyes grew used to. Having a fresh set of eyes gave our team helpful feedback into the visual and technical fixes our product needed. We refined the visuals to advertise and provide a clearer role of the chatbot on the website. Making these changes made users notice the chatbot quicker, and realize its purpose upon first glance. Conducting usability tests for my peers allowed me to take inspiration from their clean and useful design elements. I was able to put myself in the role of a user and navigate their sites with an open mind and no context. Conducting these tests allowed me to catch glitches and irregularities for my peers, which allowed them to fix issues previously unknown to them.

Week 6 · Finalizing

Delivering & Saying Goodbye

As a team, we completed our final presentation showcasing our Princess Vlei Chatbot Assistant, and our prototyped refactored website. Although our program was ending, progress was still left to be made with the website refactor. The Princess Vlei Assistant was finished and available on the old website for the time being. Our clients were pleased with our completed chatbot and the progress of our new website. They felt prioritized when we demonstrated an admin page specifically designed for the nontechnical author of the website. The admin page makes an easier transition to a new website so the client can upload and edit content in a form they are accustomed to. Being so used to using drag and drop website builders, we did not want to overwhelm the client with new technologies. Therefore, we took inspiration from these layouts to build something similar but specific for the new Princess Vlei website and their staff. Overall, saying goodbye to people I was seeing everyday for six weeks was the hardest part. Over time, I grew strong bonds with my peers. Together, we collaborated, explored, and joked. Interning abroad allowed the perfect mix of responsibility, impact, and fun. I was able to collaborate and create a project with a purpose. I was able to show up as both a peer and a friend. Watching everyone else on the program build well-thoughtout projects made me proud of the work we completed as a group.

Looking Back

Reflection

How this experience shaped me personally and professionally

The strongest lesson learned that I will continue to carry into my professional career is building by first understanding the user. Over the duration of this trip, I truly realized what human-centered computing is and how to aware of the audience when building effective products.

Interning for a nonprofit organization in South Africa allowed me to see the role I played in my client's mission. The clients old website was dated and disorganized. They had been meaning to change their website, but the task of transferring all of its content daunted them. As a team, we overcame this task through the use of AI and scripts to accurately transfer content and build a website shell. I was able to express my creativity to design a satisfying user experience. When we were creating our project, we always considered how we can build new features in a way that centers the user. This meant both the local community and Princess Vlei Staff. We wanted something simple, intuitive, and familiar. As a team, we had to ensure the chatbot, admin page, and new website had strong visual indicators and clear instructions. It felt good to disconnect from my day-to-day life in America and learn how to be open-minded and aware of cultural differences, common skillsets, and user needs. This experience taught me to listen when working in unfamiliar contexts. Additonally, I learned how to lead and collaborate with a team for an extended period of time. Thus far, this project has been my longest work. As I'm navigating my computer science career, I found this experience to be an extremely immersive way to advance my computing skills. I worked in new coding languages, developed my first website, and conducted usability tests. Having this experience provides a leg up when entering new courses, internships, and jobs. I now have a greater understanding on the software development process, collaborating with other engineers, meeting with clients to give frequent updates, and presenting project pitches. Not only did this interships provide me with professional skills, but I connected to a new people, experiences, and cultures. It felt amazing to explore new landscapes and experience an unfamilar place for six weeks. Cape Town taught me to be more open minded, curious, and out going. It was truly an eye-opening and powerful experience.

During our final presentation, a peer from another team said that coding is a very human thing because it is creating with the intention of reaching other people and being of use.

In final consideration, this study abroad internship provided me with the motivation to continue being a global citizen of the world and using my skills to build useful tools. I enjoyed creating something to make the clients workflow easier and playing a role in Princess Vlei's rich community envolvement and determination to protect the natural space. In Cape Town, I fully immersed myself in a new city and allowed myself to appreciate the unknown. Overall, I find adjusting to new enviroments and being curious easy and fufilling, and this experience played a pivotal role in that realization.

With Gratitude

Special thank you to Dr. Sanethia Thomas, Ping Neo, Naomi Harrell, EDU Africa, my Royal Gator AI team members and the Princess Vlei Forum for making this experience possible and unforgettable.
